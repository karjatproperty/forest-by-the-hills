Karjat Plot Prices: How Rates Are Actually Set

The government floor, the market ask, and the costs that turn a headline price into the real one.

Two plots a kilometre apart in Karjat can be quoted at very different rates. That isn't always a sign that one seller is wrong. Land class, road access, layout infrastructure and the view all move the number. The trick is knowing which number to compare.

The Ready Reckoner Rate

The Ready Reckoner (Annual Statement of Rates) is set by the state's registration department for each village and zone. Stamp duty is charged on the higher of this rate or the agreement value. It is a floor for tax purposes, not a market price. Buyers can look it up on the IGR Maharashtra portal by district, taluka and village before any negotiation starts.

Why Gated Plots Price Differently

A plot inside a gated layout carries the cost of internal roads, drainage, power lines, security and shared amenities. A standalone plot doesn't, but the buyer then pays for those things separately, or goes without. Comparing the two on rate alone usually misleads. What Sits on Top of the Headline Price

  • Stamp duty, calculated on the higher of agreement value or Ready Reckoner value
  • Registration charges
  • Development or infrastructure charges in gated layouts
  • Maintenance deposits, where a layout has common areas
  • GST, where it applies to development components
  • Legal fees for the title search

Before Negotiating

Pull the Ready Reckoner rate for the village. Ask whether the quoted price includes development charges. Get the plot area confirmed against the layout plan, not just the sales sheet. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Ready Reckoner rate used for in Karjat?

It is the government valuation used to calculate the minimum stamp duty. Stamp duty is charged on the higher of the Ready Reckoner value or the agreement value.

How many sq. ft. are in one guntha?

One guntha is about 1,089 sq. ft., or roughly 101 sq. m. Forty guntha make one acre.

Why are gated plots priced higher per sq. ft.?

The rate includes internal roads, utilities, security and shared amenities. A standalone plot buyer pays for these separately or goes without them.
